# Art review — "Hope, and the Light"

## What was reviewed

Every work that had actually arrived on disk was opened and looked at, not judged
from its record. At the time of writing that is **153 images**: `img/full/<slug>.jpg`
and `meta/<slug>.json` agreed exactly, one record per image, so no arrived picture
was skipped and no missing one was guessed. (The manifest was stale at 74 entries;
the `meta/` records and the image directory were the working list.)

**Reviewed: 153. Kept: 115. Dropped: 38.** Average score of the kept works: 7.5.

## The ten worst offenders

1. **caricature-of-cubism** (2) — a line cartoon strip mocking cubism, a joke at the movement's expense, not a picture.
2. **cubist-costumes-for-salome** (2) — photographs of theatre costume designs; documentation of costumes, no painting.
3. **cubist-salome-by-wilde** (2) — a press photograph of an actress in costume, a production still.
4. **kamerny-theatre-curtain** (2) — a photograph of a painted theatre curtain, captioned like a slide.
5. **psyhetozy-pdf** (2) — a 1922 futurist book cover; a printed title page with lettering.
6. **rozanova-cropped** (2) — a black-and-white studio photograph of the artist, not a work.
7. **rozanova** (2) — another sepia studio photograph of the artist, not a work.
8. **stijl-vol-04-nr-07-p-107-108** (2) — a printed magazine page of Kurt Schwitters type poems; text, not painting.
9. **stijl-vol-05-nr-06-p-081-082** (2) — a De Stijl magazine page with an article and a small cut reproduction.
10. **trotsky-annenkow-1922-cartoon** (2) — an ink political cartoon of Trotsky, a newspaper joke drawing.

Also at the bottom: the Augsburg *Wunderzeichenbuch* folio, the Meuse Bible
page, the handwritten De Stijl pupil list, the Horațiu Dimitriu caricature, and the
Frans Zwollo silver-bowl photograph (all score 2); and at score 3 the
*art-concret* magazine centrefold, the Carl Bittmann lithograph poster, the
Sant'Elia architectural drawing, the Russel Wright circus-costume photograph, the
Lissitzky photographic self-portrait, the Boccioni sculpture photographs, the

book cover (`file-jpg`), and the Malevich portrait reproduced in black and white.

## What the junk has in common

The 38 drops are not bad paintings; they are not paintings at all. They fall into a
few families: photographs of people or objects (Rozanova twice, Lissitzky, the Zwollo
silver bowl, the Boccioni sculptures, the Salome costumes), printed matter
(magazine pages, book covers, posters, exhibition catalogues, manuscript folios), and
drawings that document something else (architectural perspectives, theatre curtains,
caricatures, cartoons). A second, smaller group are monochrome reproductions of real
paintings — the street pavers photograph, the Malevich *Portrait of Ivan Kliun*, the
1906 Van Doesburg still life, the 1921 Red Army painting, the Popova untitled — where
the picture is only being shown as evidence of a work that exists elsewhere.

## Rule for the selection next time

**Show only the work itself.** Before a slug is allowed into the gallery, the image
must be a finished painting, or a finished drawing or print, by the named artist,
photographed in colour and filling the frame. Reject on sight: photographs of people,
objects, interiors, sculptures or stages; costume and set designs; theatre curtains;
posters and book covers; magazine and catalogue pages; manuscript and archive scans;
architectural drawings and diagrams; caricatures and cartoons; and any black-and-white
or otherwise monochrome reproduction of a painting, because it documents a picture
rather than showing one. When a work exists only as such a reproduction, leave it out
and find the colour image instead.
