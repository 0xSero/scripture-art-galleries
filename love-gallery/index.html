Love, Abstracted

Love is difficult to paint directly, so most of the works here approach it sideways. The figure is never abandoned, but it is continually on the way to becoming something else — a vortex of limbs, a single silhouette, a block of marble that has not finished releasing two bodies, a gold pattern in which two faces are almost lost.

The selection below follows that bias: works where abstraction and the human survive together. They are arranged from allegory, through Symbolism, line and carved mass, to the threshold of modernity. Every image is public domain or CC0, reproduced from Wikimedia Commons, and stored locally beside this page so the gallery works offline. Each plate carries a psalm verse set into the image itself, in the King James Version. Click any plate to enlarge it; use the arrow keys to move through the catalogue.

I

Allegory and Emblem

Love as an argument rather than an event: figures arranged so that the idea outweighs the bodies carrying it, from Botticelli's weather to Veronese's knot.

II

The Symbolist Dream

The figure kept, the flesh withdrawn. Faces are withheld, doubled, or replaced, and longing is handed over to colour, flower and dream.

III

Line and Gesture

Bodies reduced to contour. A few strokes of chalk, copper or pencil carry the whole of tenderness, and nothing else is needed.

IV

The Body as Form

Two people carved as one mass, from Canova's linked graces to Rodin's lovers pulled apart in mid-air. The most literal subject made the most sculptural.

V

The Threshold of Modernity

The human dissolved on purpose: into gold, into pattern, into a single unreadable feature, and still unmistakably a person.
